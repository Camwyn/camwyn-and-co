If you pick up a well-made book from a century ago, turn past the final chapter, and look at the quiet page facing the rear endpaper, you will often find a short paragraph set in modest type. 

It will tell you that the volume was set in Caslon by a printer in Boston, printed on rag paper from a particular Massachusetts mill, and bound using linen thread under the care of a named foreman. That little note is a colophon—from the Greek word for *finishing touch* or *summit*. 

It was never an advertisement. It was an acknowledgment. It told the reader that this object did not materialize out of thin air. It was made by real hands, using specific materials, in a physical room with south-facing windows and wood dust on the sills.

Somewhere in the transition to digital screens, the web lost its habit of leaving a colophon.

## The Mirage of the Seamless Surface

Most modern software strives to appear frictionless, weightless, and seamless. Entire industries are built around hiding the seams: bundling thousands of unseen dependencies into an opaque blob, running background trackers that observe without asking, and presenting interfaces that pretend they have no physical or structural cost.

When an interface hides its construction, it also hides its compromises. It encourages us to treat software as disposable—something to be consumed quickly, discarded easily, and rebuilt from scratch every eighteen months when the tooling trends shift.

We wanted this corner of the internet to feel different. 

At Camwyn & Co, we believe that **smart can be warm** and that **serious work can still be fun**. If you spend hours tuning the kerning of a heading or calculating whether an accent color passes AAA contrast under dim evening light, that care shouldn't be swept under the rug. It should be acknowledged.

## What Goes Into the Workshop

That is why we maintain a dedicated Colophon. We don't believe you should have to hunt through fine print in the footer to find out how this place was built—you can **[turn directly to our Colophon&nbsp;&rarr;](/colophon/)**.

Inside, we step out from behind the counter to show you what this house is made of:

1. **The Type:** We tell you that our headings are set in *DM Serif Display*, drawn by Colophon Foundry; that our body prose breathes through *Hanken Grotesk*, designed by Alfredo Marco Pradil; and that our timestamps and data markers click into place with *JetBrains Mono*. Typefaces carry voice and temperature. Naming their designers is simple courtesy.
2. **The Palette:** We publish our hex codes and contrast ratios openly—Coffee Black, Warm Sand, Paper White, and Muted Clay—not as corporate brand guidelines, but as the pigments mixed on our workbench.
3. **The Joinery:** We explain why we chose Eleventy over heavy client-side JavaScript frameworks, why our CSS is written by hand in layered object-oriented files with zero Tailwind runtime, and why every button and container has a strict 0px border radius like the trimmed edge of printed paper.
4. **The Respect:** We document our zero-tracking policy. No marketing pixels, no cookie consent modals, and no behavioral analytics watching over your shoulder while you read.

## Hospitality Begins with Honesty

A good table is generous, but it is also honest. When a host pulls down a bottle of single malt and tells you about the coastal distillery, the peat smoke, or the sherry cask where the spirit rested for a dozen winters in the salt air, it isn't pretension—it is gratitude for the craft. It deepens the conversation.

A colophon does the same thing for a website. It reminds both the maker and the visitor that a digital space can be an atelier rather than a funnel. 

It says: *We cared enough about this place to build it with intention. Here is how it was done.*

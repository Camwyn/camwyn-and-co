When I finally returned to technology at the age of 42, I arrived with a "Threshold View" that only a lifetime of shifts could provide. I didn't just bring the ability to write PHP or architect WordPress plugins; I brought the **Human API**.

After years of managing high-velocity kitchens and analyzing the structural integrity of commercial real estate, I realized that the most complex systems aren't made of steel or syntax—they are made of people.

### Building the Failover Protocols

During my time at **Modern Tribe** and **StellarWP**, my focus shifted from being the person writing the code to being the person designing the **People Systems**. I specialized in taking fragmented remote teams and turning them into cohesive, high-output squads.

I learned that:

- **Diversity is Optimization:** Inclusion isn't a checklist; it is the ultimate fail-safe against groupthink.

- **Psychological Safety is Infrastructure:** A team can only reach peak velocity if they have the "failover protocols" to know they are safe when things break.

- **Sustainability over Shortcuts:** Much like the endangered livestock I raise today, the best architecture takes time to mature.

### Reciprocity and the Great Pivot

The heart of this journey has been a masterclass in reciprocity with my wife, Ivy. We took turns supporting each other through our respective educations and career pivots. When life forced a transition to the North Country, we didn't just "buy a farm"; we built a resilient system that could sustain us through chronic illness and career shifts.

### Sitting on the Porch

Today, when I stand on the porch with **Rue**, surveying the Yaks and Meishan pigs at **Mema’s Inclination**, I see a singular, unified logic. The "tinkerer’s mindset" that keeps my motorcycles running is the same one that diagnoses friction points in a corporate department.

I am moving away from the keyboard and toward the mission. I am looking for my next leadership challenge—one that requires a leader who understands that whether you are managing a 501(c)(3) rescue, a global dev team, or a Friday night pizza rush, you aren't just managing tickets.

**You are managing the mission.**

To reclaim time for my two daughters, I made a pivot that, on the surface, seemed a world away from the high-velocity kitchens of my past. I entered the world of **Real Estate Appraisal**.

Working alongside my father, I transitioned from residential assessments to complex commercial appraisal. For a decade, I didn't just look at buildings; I analyzed the "bones" of physical and financial systems. I was tasked with looking past the fresh coat of paint to evaluate income streams, cap rates, and structural integrity.

### Integrity-Based Analysis

This was my decade of **Integrity-Based Analysis**. In appraisal, you learn quickly that you cannot just _say_ a property has value; you have to show the data that proves it.

This era instilled in me a rigorous mental framework that I carry into leadership today:

- **Evidence over Assumption:** Don't tell me the project is "on track"; show me the velocity metrics and the "structural" health of the code.

- **The Interconnectedness of Variables:** Just as a single zoning change can sink a property’s value, a single "breaking change" in a WordPress ecosystem can sink a product line.

- **Proactive Problem Solving:** During these years, I served as the tech head of the NH chapter of NAIFA, building their digital infrastructure from scratch because I saw a system that needed a better "foundation".

### The Data-Modeling Mindset

When I finally made my "Great Pivot" back to tech at age 42, I wasn't starting from zero. I was simply moving from modeling the data of physical buildings to modeling the data of digital ones.

Whether I’m redesigning my barn in Burke to accommodate a specific workflow for my pigs or architecting a failover protocol for a remote dev team, I am looking for **Structural Integrity**. I’ve learned that a system is only as strong as its underlying data and its resilience when the "market" shifts.

I don’t just look at the code. I look at the bones.

Ideas in isolation are delicate things. They get stuck in notebooks, over-analyzed on whiteboards, and deferred until "the timing is right."

To turn an idea into a living thing, you need momentum. And momentum almost always comes from bringing two different minds together.

## The Chemistry of Collaboration

The best partnerships are not made between people who think alike; they are made between people whose different strengths fit together like joinery:

- One brings the spark, the other brings the hearth.
- One asks *"what if we start right now?"*, the other asks *"how do we make it hold weight?"*
- One sees the horizon, the other sees the joinery.

> "A great collaborator is someone who gives your best instincts permission to act."

When that alignment exists, a project moves from hesitation to inevitability.

## Three Catalytic Habits

1. **Send the message early:** Don't wait until the pitch deck is perfect. Start a conversation with a single clear sentence.
2. **Match energy with action:** If someone shares excitement about an idea, build the smallest real version of it within 48 hours.
3. **Protect generosity:** Great ventures are built on shared credit, open doors, and mutual enthusiasm.

Small spark, steady fire, worthwhile adventure.
